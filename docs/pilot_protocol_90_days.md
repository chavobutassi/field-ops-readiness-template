# 90-day pilot protocol

A pilot is a way to measure, not a promise of results. Its purpose is to let the organization decide with evidence whether the routine is worth keeping.

## Before you start

- Agree in writing on the sites, areas and people included. Two or three sites are enough.
- Use only the data you need. Do not include personal identifiers beyond names and certificate dates.
- Name one person on the organization's side who owns the pilot.

## Five measures

| Measure | Better when |
|---|---|
| Readiness index | Higher |
| Median first-response time (minutes, last 30 days) | Lower |
| Overdue work orders | Fewer |
| Sites with an open violation | Fewer |
| Certificates expiring within 60 days | Fewer |

## Phases

| Days | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 30 | Baseline | Load the data, record the starting values, run the weekly routine once |
| 31 to 60 | Routine | Run the routine every week and record the five measures |
| 61 to 90 | Review | Compare the baseline with the latest week and decide whether to continue |

## Suggested targets

- Readiness index up 10 points, or at least 80.
- Median response down 20%.
- Two or fewer overdue work orders.
- Open violations cut in half.
- No certificate expiring within 60 days.

Targets are for discussion. If conditions change, write down why and adjust.
